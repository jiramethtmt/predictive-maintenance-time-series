Why not just take the likeliest class

Only 2.7% of trucks are inside a failure window, so the most likely class is almost always zero and the model never raises an alarm. Switch the rule above and the cost jumps straight back to the do-nothing baseline.

Why checking everything is not stupid

Under the challenge costs, sending the whole fleet in blind beats the do-nothing policy, 49,671 against 56,100. That is the bar a model has to clear, not accuracy.

What the model actually reads

Cumulative counters converted to wear rates, six usage histograms normalised to shapes, how irregularly the truck reports, and eight anonymised spec fields. No raw sensor names: Scania released the data anonymised.

Data: SCANIA Component X, Scania CV AB, CC BY 4.0, via the Swedish National Data Service (10.5878/jvb5-d390). Model trained on cut points sampled from the 18,639 training trucks that have telemetry, evaluated on the held-out test split.
